Buri

What it is and how it is used in weaving.

Corypha or the gebang palm, buri palm or talipot palm 
is a genus of palms (family Arecaceae), native to India, 
Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, New Guinea and 
northeastern Australia (Cape York Peninsula, Queensland). 
They are fan palms (subfamily Coryphoideae), and the leaves 
have a long petiole terminating in a rounded fan of numerous 
leaflets. (Wikiwand)
									
The history of buri and weaving is deeply rooted in the 
cultural identity of various regions in the Philippines.
The buri palm leaves are carefully harvested, dried, and
woven into intricate patterns, creating beautiful mats,
baskets, and other handicrafts.

Process

Behind every creation is a purpose, a vision, and a passion for turning simple ideas into something meaningful.

1. Preparation of Materials: Dried Buri was purchased to make the process more efficient and convenient.
2. Individual Weaving: Each group member was assigned an individual weaving task to ensure that the work was completed efficiently.
3. Painting and Assembly: Some members were assigned to paint the woven pieces, while others worked on assembling the pparts to create the final product.

Documentation

Editing of the PAMANA's procedure and documentation.

As we finish this project, we look back on everything we learned and experienced as a group. Making buri-woven products was not always easy. We faced challenges in weaving the buri, making our products neat, and putting the different parts together. There were times when we had to fix our work and try again, but we helped one another and did our best to finish the project. Through this experience, we learned to be more patient, creative, and responsible, and we realized how iimportant teamwork is in reaching our goal. This project also helped us appreciate the history of buri weaving and its importance in Filipino culture. Creating our own products and making a website to show the history and process mades us realize that we can help keep this tradition alive in our own way. We are proud of what we accomplished because we did not only create products, but also learned something meaningful about our product. Even though we faced difficulties, we were able to overcome them together. We hope that thrrough work, more people will learn about buri weaving, appreciate the effort behind it, and continue this tradition for future generations.
